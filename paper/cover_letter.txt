Dear Editors of the World Wide Web journal,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript "When Should Web-RAG Refresh?
Adapting Refresh Policies Across Drift Regimes" for consideration as a
regular research article.

Web-backed retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems cache retrieved
pages to cut latency and cost, but cached content grows stale against
the live web. Our manuscript studies the per-query refresh decision —
answer from the cache, or pay for a live re-fetch — and shows that the
answer depends on the corpus drift regime. The work fits the journal's
scope on web information systems and web data management, and makes
three contributions:

1. A two-regime empirical framework (FreshQA low-drift vs. our new
   TimeVarying high-drift benchmark, released with the code) with a
   controlled channel decomposition of the counter-intuitive finding
   that blind Always-Refresh is the worst policy in low drift.

2. AdaRefresh, a transfer-and-adapt recipe for proxy-trained refresh
   routers: a collapsed zero-shot router recovers to F1 0.685 with as
   few as four labeled target queries, confirmed by a from-scratch
   control arm and leave-one-category-out validation.

3. RegimeTest, a corpus-level regime classifier that separates the two
   drift regimes from ~20 unlabeled deployment queries and selects the
   policy family matching (or within 0.02 of) the best fixed policy.

All experiments compare against five internal policies plus three
external baselines adapted to the refresh setting (Random-p%, TTL, and
a Self-RAG-style [RETRIEVE] router). Code, data, and the benchmark are
released for reproducibility.

This manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is
not under consideration elsewhere. All authors have approved the
submission and declare no conflicts of interest.
